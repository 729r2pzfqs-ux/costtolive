Cost of Living Overview

Anchorage is more expensive than the national average. Higher housing costs are typically the primary driver. With a cost index of 113 (where 100 equals the national median), Anchorage residents can expect to pay median rent of $1,453/month while earning a median household income of $98,152/year.

The median home value in Anchorage, AK is $375,900, with typical monthly owner costs around $1,698 including mortgage, insurance, and taxes. The median rent-to-income ratio of 17.8% means housing is relatively affordable here — well below the 30% threshold that HUD considers cost-burdened.

Alaska has no state income tax, which can represent significant savings compared to high-tax states. Combined with a 0% sales tax rate and 1.04% effective property tax rate, the overall tax environment helps keep costs manageable in Anchorage.

Common questions about living costs in Anchorage

What is the cost of living in Anchorage, AK?
The cost of living index in Anchorage, AK is 113 (US average = 100). Median monthly rent is $1,453, median household income is $98,152/year, and the median home value is $375,900.
Is Anchorage, AK affordable?
No, Anchorage is more expensive than the national average with a cost index of 113. The rent-to-income ratio is 17.8%—below the 30% threshold considered affordable by HUD.
What is the average rent in Anchorage, AK?

What is the median household income in Anchorage?
The median household income in Anchorage, AK is $98,152 per year. This compares to the national median of approximately $80,000.
What are the taxes like in Alaska?
Alaska has a top income tax rate of 0%, a sales tax of 0%, and an effective property tax rate of 1.04%. Notably, there is no state income tax.
