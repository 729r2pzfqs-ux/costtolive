Cost of Living Overview

Everett has a significantly higher cost of living than most US cities, driven largely by premium housing costs. With a cost index of 137 (where 100 equals the national median), Everett residents can expect to pay median rent of $1,675/month while earning a median household income of $81,502/year.

The median home value in Everett, WA is $532,300, with typical monthly owner costs around $1,738 including mortgage, insurance, and taxes. The median rent-to-income ratio of 24.7% means housing is relatively affordable here — well below the 30% threshold that HUD considers cost-burdened.

Washington has no state income tax, which can represent significant savings compared to high-tax states. Combined with a 6.5% sales tax rate and 0.92% effective property tax rate, the overall tax environment helps keep costs manageable in Everett.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about living costs in Everett

What is the cost of living in Everett, WA?
The cost of living index in Everett, WA is 137 (US average = 100). Median monthly rent is $1,675, median household income is $81,502/year, and the median home value is $532,300.
Is Everett, WA affordable?
No, Everett is more expensive than the national average with a cost index of 137. The rent-to-income ratio is 24.7%—below the 30% threshold considered affordable by HUD.
What is the average rent in Everett, WA?

What is the median household income in Everett?
The median household income in Everett, WA is $81,502 per year. This compares to the national median of approximately $80,000.
What are the taxes like in Washington?
Washington has a top income tax rate of 0%, a sales tax of 6.5%, and an effective property tax rate of 0.92%. Notably, there is no state income tax.
