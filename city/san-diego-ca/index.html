Cost of Living Overview

San Diego has a significantly higher cost of living than most US cities, driven largely by premium housing costs. With a cost index of 197 (where 100 equals the national median), San Diego residents can expect to pay median rent of $2,223/month while earning a median household income of $104,321/year.

The median home value in San Diego, CA is $848,500, with typical monthly owner costs around $2,308 including mortgage, insurance, and taxes. With a rent-to-income ratio of 25.6%, San Diego falls within the generally affordable range, though it's approaching the 30% burden threshold.

California's top income tax rate of 13.3% is among the highest in the nation, adding to the overall cost burden. Combined with a 7.25% sales tax rate and 0.71% effective property tax rate, the overall tax environment is a factor in the total cost of living in San Diego.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about living costs in San Diego

What is the cost of living in San Diego, CA?
The cost of living index in San Diego, CA is 197 (US average = 100). Median monthly rent is $2,223, median household income is $104,321/year, and the median home value is $848,500.
Is San Diego, CA affordable?
No, San Diego is more expensive than the national average with a cost index of 197. The rent-to-income ratio is 25.6%—below the 30% threshold considered affordable by HUD.
What is the average rent in San Diego, CA?
The median gross rent in San Diego, CA is $2,223 per month according to the US Census Bureau ACS 2023 data. Annual rent costs approximately $26,676.
What is the median household income in San Diego?
The median household income in San Diego, CA is $104,321 per year. This compares to the national median of approximately $80,000.
What are the taxes like in California?
California has a top income tax rate of 13.3%, a sales tax of 7.25%, and an effective property tax rate of 0.71%.
