Cost of Living Overview

Fort Worth has a cost of living close to the national average, offering a balanced mix of expenses and amenities. With a cost index of 97 (where 100 equals the national median), Fort Worth residents can expect to pay median rent of $1,412/month while earning a median household income of $76,602/year.

The median home value in Fort Worth, TX is $277,300, with typical monthly owner costs around $1,489 including mortgage, insurance, and taxes. The median rent-to-income ratio of 22.1% means housing is relatively affordable here — well below the 30% threshold that HUD considers cost-burdened.

Texas has no state income tax, which can represent significant savings compared to high-tax states. Combined with a 6.25% sales tax rate and 1.6% effective property tax rate, the overall tax environment helps keep costs manageable in Fort Worth.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about living costs in Fort Worth

What is the cost of living in Fort Worth, TX?
The cost of living index in Fort Worth, TX is 97 (US average = 100). Median monthly rent is $1,412, median household income is $76,602/year, and the median home value is $277,300.
Is Fort Worth, TX affordable?
Yes, Fort Worth is more affordable than the national average with a cost index of 97. The rent-to-income ratio is 22.1%—below the 30% threshold considered affordable by HUD.
What is the average rent in Fort Worth, TX?
The median gross rent in Fort Worth, TX is $1,412 per month according to the US Census Bureau ACS 2023 data. Annual rent costs approximately $16,944.
What is the median household income in Fort Worth?
The median household income in Fort Worth, TX is $76,602 per year. This compares to the national median of approximately $80,000.
What are the taxes like in Texas?
Texas has a top income tax rate of 0%, a sales tax of 6.25%, and an effective property tax rate of 1.6%. Notably, there is no state income tax.
