Cost of Living Overview

Nashville is more expensive than the national average. Higher housing costs are typically the primary driver. With a cost index of 110 (where 100 equals the national median), Nashville residents can expect to pay median rent of $1,486/month while earning a median household income of $75,197/year.

The median home value in Nashville, TN is $383,100, with typical monthly owner costs around $1,471 including mortgage, insurance, and taxes. The median rent-to-income ratio of 23.7% means housing is relatively affordable here — well below the 30% threshold that HUD considers cost-burdened.

Tennessee has no state income tax, which can represent significant savings compared to high-tax states. Combined with a 7.0% sales tax rate and 0.64% effective property tax rate, the overall tax environment helps keep costs manageable in Nashville.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about living costs in Nashville

What is the cost of living in Nashville, TN?
The cost of living index in Nashville, TN is 110 (US average = 100). Median monthly rent is $1,486, median household income is $75,197/year, and the median home value is $383,100.
Is Nashville, TN affordable?
No, Nashville is more expensive than the national average with a cost index of 110. The rent-to-income ratio is 23.7%—below the 30% threshold considered affordable by HUD.
What is the average rent in Nashville, TN?
The median gross rent in Nashville, TN is $1,486 per month according to the US Census Bureau ACS 2023 data. Annual rent costs approximately $17,832.
What is the median household income in Nashville?
The median household income in Nashville, TN is $75,197 per year. This compares to the national median of approximately $80,000.
What are the taxes like in Tennessee?
Tennessee has a top income tax rate of 0%, a sales tax of 7.0%, and an effective property tax rate of 0.64%. Notably, there is no state income tax.
