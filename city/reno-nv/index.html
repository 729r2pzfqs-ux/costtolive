Cost of Living Overview

Reno has a significantly higher cost of living than most US cities, driven largely by premium housing costs. With a cost index of 122 (where 100 equals the national median), Reno residents can expect to pay median rent of $1,453/month while earning a median household income of $78,448/year.

The median home value in Reno, NV is $498,600, with typical monthly owner costs around $1,482 including mortgage, insurance, and taxes. The median rent-to-income ratio of 22.2% means housing is relatively affordable here — well below the 30% threshold that HUD considers cost-burdened.

Nevada has no state income tax, which can represent significant savings compared to high-tax states. Combined with a 6.85% sales tax rate and 0.53% effective property tax rate, the overall tax environment helps keep costs manageable in Reno.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about living costs in Reno

What is the cost of living in Reno, NV?
The cost of living index in Reno, NV is 122 (US average = 100). Median monthly rent is $1,453, median household income is $78,448/year, and the median home value is $498,600.
Is Reno, NV affordable?
No, Reno is more expensive than the national average with a cost index of 122. The rent-to-income ratio is 22.2%—below the 30% threshold considered affordable by HUD.
What is the average rent in Reno, NV?
The median gross rent in Reno, NV is $1,453 per month according to the US Census Bureau ACS 2023 data. Annual rent costs approximately $17,436.
What is the median household income in Reno?
The median household income in Reno, NV is $78,448 per year. This compares to the national median of approximately $80,000.
What are the taxes like in Nevada?
Nevada has a top income tax rate of 0%, a sales tax of 6.85%, and an effective property tax rate of 0.53%. Notably, there is no state income tax.
