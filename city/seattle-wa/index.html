Cost of Living Overview

Seattle has a significantly higher cost of living than most US cities, driven largely by premium housing costs. With a cost index of 196 (where 100 equals the national median), Seattle residents can expect to pay median rent of $1,998/month while earning a median household income of $121,984/year.

The median home value in Seattle, WA is $912,100, with typical monthly owner costs around $2,220 including mortgage, insurance, and taxes. The median rent-to-income ratio of 19.7% means housing is relatively affordable here — well below the 30% threshold that HUD considers cost-burdened.

Washington has no state income tax, which can represent significant savings compared to high-tax states. Combined with a 6.5% sales tax rate and 0.92% effective property tax rate, the overall tax environment helps keep costs manageable in Seattle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about living costs in Seattle

What is the cost of living in Seattle, WA?
The cost of living index in Seattle, WA is 196 (US average = 100). Median monthly rent is $1,998, median household income is $121,984/year, and the median home value is $912,100.
Is Seattle, WA affordable?
No, Seattle is more expensive than the national average with a cost index of 196. The rent-to-income ratio is 19.7%—below the 30% threshold considered affordable by HUD.
What is the average rent in Seattle, WA?
The median gross rent in Seattle, WA is $1,998 per month according to the US Census Bureau ACS 2023 data. Annual rent costs approximately $23,976.
What is the median household income in Seattle?
The median household income in Seattle, WA is $121,984 per year. This compares to the national median of approximately $80,000.
What are the taxes like in Washington?
Washington has a top income tax rate of 0%, a sales tax of 6.5%, and an effective property tax rate of 0.92%. Notably, there is no state income tax.
