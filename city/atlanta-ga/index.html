Cost of Living Overview

Atlanta has a significantly higher cost of living than most US cities, driven largely by premium housing costs. With a cost index of 122 (where 100 equals the national median), Atlanta residents can expect to pay median rent of $1,617/month while earning a median household income of $81,938/year.

The median home value in Atlanta, GA is $420,600, with typical monthly owner costs around $1,695 including mortgage, insurance, and taxes. The median rent-to-income ratio of 23.7% means housing is relatively affordable here — well below the 30% threshold that HUD considers cost-burdened.

Georgia has a moderate income tax rate of 5.49%. Combined with a 4.0% sales tax rate and 0.92% effective property tax rate, the overall tax environment is a factor in the total cost of living in Atlanta.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about living costs in Atlanta

What is the cost of living in Atlanta, GA?
The cost of living index in Atlanta, GA is 122 (US average = 100). Median monthly rent is $1,617, median household income is $81,938/year, and the median home value is $420,600.
Is Atlanta, GA affordable?
No, Atlanta is more expensive than the national average with a cost index of 122. The rent-to-income ratio is 23.7%—below the 30% threshold considered affordable by HUD.
What is the average rent in Atlanta, GA?
The median gross rent in Atlanta, GA is $1,617 per month according to the US Census Bureau ACS 2023 data. Annual rent costs approximately $19,404.
What is the median household income in Atlanta?
The median household income in Atlanta, GA is $81,938 per year. This compares to the national median of approximately $80,000.
What are the taxes like in Georgia?
Georgia has a top income tax rate of 5.49%, a sales tax of 4.0%, and an effective property tax rate of 0.92%.
