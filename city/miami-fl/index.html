Cost of Living Overview

Miami has a significantly higher cost of living than most US cities, driven largely by premium housing costs. With a cost index of 129 (where 100 equals the national median), Miami residents can expect to pay median rent of $1,657/month while earning a median household income of $59,390/year.

The median home value in Miami, FL is $475,200, with typical monthly owner costs around $1,666 including mortgage, insurance, and taxes. At 33.5% rent-to-income ratio, residents of Miami are spending more than the recommended 30% of income on housing, indicating moderate cost burden.

Florida has no state income tax, which can represent significant savings compared to high-tax states. Combined with a 6.0% sales tax rate and 0.86% effective property tax rate, the overall tax environment helps keep costs manageable in Miami.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about living costs in Miami

What is the cost of living in Miami, FL?
The cost of living index in Miami, FL is 129 (US average = 100). Median monthly rent is $1,657, median household income is $59,390/year, and the median home value is $475,200.
Is Miami, FL affordable?
No, Miami is more expensive than the national average with a cost index of 129. The rent-to-income ratio is 33.5%—above the 30% affordability threshold.
What is the average rent in Miami, FL?
The median gross rent in Miami, FL is $1,657 per month according to the US Census Bureau ACS 2023 data. Annual rent costs approximately $19,884.
What is the median household income in Miami?
The median household income in Miami, FL is $59,390 per year. This compares to the national median of approximately $80,000.
What are the taxes like in Florida?
Florida has a top income tax rate of 0%, a sales tax of 6.0%, and an effective property tax rate of 0.86%. Notably, there is no state income tax.
